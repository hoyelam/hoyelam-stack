# Continuity record

Use this shape when a pause, handoff, or reconstruction needs a durable record. Adapt it to the work; omit fields that do not help the next owner. Reuse the project's existing record instead of introducing another tracking system.

- Objective, current status, and remaining work in dependency order.
- Repository, branch, revision, relevant uncommitted changes, and evidence locations that identify the actual state.
- Decisions and constraints that affect the next action, including existing authority and material rejected approaches.
- Verification performed and observed results, including failures, blocked checks, and evidence that must be refreshed. Include reproducible commands or interactions when useful.
- The first useful command, file, or decision on resumption and any external blocker that prevents it.

Keep the record compact and link to supporting evidence. Do not copy whole transcripts, secrets, or unrelated task history into it. A checkpoint can preserve unfinished or failing work; distinguish that from a completed outcome.

Before resuming, compare the record with live state. A revision alone does not describe a dirty worktree, and an old pass does not cover later relevant changes. Continue from the first unverified step without repeating checks whose evidence remains current.
